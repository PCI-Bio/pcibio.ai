FAQ

Questions labs ask first.

What does the software do?

It grades every tumor nucleus on a p53 slide from 0 to 3+ against the three control lines stained on the same slide, scores it against established, published p53 criteria and reports the pattern: wild-type, overexpression, null, or borderline for the pathologist's review. A pathologist reviews the result and signs.

Which stains does it read?

p53 today. Mismatch repair (MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, MSH6) is in development.

Which slides and scanners work?

Aperio SVS and TIFF whole-slide images today. Other scanner formats on request.

Who places the regions?

The laboratory confirms the three control lines on the slide. The pathologist marks the tumor region to score. The software suggests both; a person confirms them.

How long does a case take?

Most cases are analysed in about half an hour, and three in four in under 45 minutes, once the slides are uploaded and the regions marked. The case shows as ready for review when it is done.

What happens if the slide isn't good enough?

The case gets no call and one plain sentence saying why: the control lines didn't stain, there were too few tumor nuclei, or the two slides couldn't be aligned.

Do I need PCI's control blocks?

The analysis reads three control lines with known p53 staining on the slide. PCI's TriControl FFPE blocks provide them, cut and stained with the tissue.

Can it be used for diagnosis?

No. It is for research use only and not for use in diagnostic procedures.

Where does patient data go?

Nowhere it doesn't need to. Slide labels are removed at upload, the requisition takes a case code instead of a name, and each laboratory sees only its own cases.
