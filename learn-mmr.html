Section 01

What the mismatch repair system does

Every time a cell divides it copies its whole genome, roughly three billion base pairs. Errors are inevitable. The mismatch repair (MMR) system is the cell's proofreader: proteins that scan newly copied DNA, find mistakes and fix them before they become permanent mutations.

The four proteins that matter clinically are MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PMS2. They work in pairs called heterodimers: MLH1 bonds with PMS2, MSH2 bonds with MSH6. The pairs are interdependent. When one partner is lost, the other becomes unstable and is usually lost with it. That pairing is one of the most useful things a pathologist looks for on an MMR panel, because it narrows down which gene is defective.

When the system breaks down, through an inherited mutation or an acquired one, replication errors accumulate unchecked. The result is microsatellite instability (MSI), the hallmark of MMR-deficient tumors. MMR deficiency is found in roughly 15 % of colorectal cancers and is common in endometrial, gastric and other cancers.

Section 02

How IHC reads the four proteins

Unlike p53, with its three patterns, MMR IHC is read as a binary: proficient (pMMR) or deficient (dMMR).

Intact (pMMR). Strong nuclear staining in the tumor cells for all four proteins. A tumor is called proficient only when all four are intact. If even one is lost, the tumor is deficient.

Lost (dMMR). Complete absence of nuclear staining in the tumor cells for one or more proteins, while the surrounding normal tissue stays stained, which is what confirms the stain itself worked. Loss of one protein almost always pulls its partner down with it.

Section 03

Clinical relevance

MMR status has a direct, immediate effect on patient care in two areas.

1 · Lynch syndrome screening

Lynch syndrome is an inherited condition caused by a germline mutation in one of the four MMR genes. It accounts for a notable share of colorectal cancers and raises the lifetime risk of endometrial, gastric, urinary tract and other cancers. Finding dMMR on IHC is often the first step toward a Lynch evaluation, above all when the panel shows isolated PMS2 loss or MSH2 and MSH6 loss, patterns that sporadic methylation rarely causes.

2 · Immunotherapy eligibility

dMMR, or high microsatellite instability (MSI-H), is a key biomarker for immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy, which works by lifting the brakes on the immune system's attack on the tumor. Checkpoint inhibitors are approved for dMMR and MSI-H tumors across cancer types regardless of where the tumor began, so an accurate MMR read is tied directly to whether a patient receives that treatment.
